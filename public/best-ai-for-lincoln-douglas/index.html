The LD clock, and what covers each part

Lincoln-Douglas is one debater against one, on a value resolution. The National Speech & Debate Association times the round at 6-3-7-3-4-6-3, with four minutes of prep each; the Lincoln-Douglas guide explains the rules. Here is that clock, segment by segment, with the part of DebateOwl that covers it and the places it does not.

  1. AC6:00
    Affirmative constructive

    DebateOwl writes the affirmative as a speech you can read out, with "If they say… we answer…" replies after it. Sparring gives you the six minutes to deliver it.

  2. CX3:00
    Negative questions the affirmative

    Crossfire, with the AI asking: one question at a time about your case, on a 3-minute clock.

    Sparring skips this period.

  3. NC7:00
    Negative constructive

    When you are affirmative, the AI gives this speech in Sparring. When you are negative, Attack reads the affirmative case and points to its weakest link.

  4. CX3:00
    Affirmative questions the negative

    Crossfire the other way round: you ask, and you choose whether the AI answers cooperatively, evasively or aggressively.

    Sparring skips this period too.

  5. 1AR4:00
    First affirmative rebuttal

    The speech most LD rounds turn on. Sparring makes you give it in four minutes against the seven-minute NC the AI just delivered.

  6. NR6:00
    Negative rebuttal

    In Sparring, the AI's last speech when you are affirmative, or your chance to collapse onto one clean line when you are negative.

  7. 2AR3:00
    Second affirmative rebuttal

    The last word, then the verdict: a score in four categories and a note on what decided the round.

  8. Prep4:00
    Each debater, between speeches

    The free LD timer keeps both prep banks.

    Sparring has no prep clock.

A value and criterion, tested before the round

An example resolution, not a current NSDA topic: "Resolved: A just society ought to guarantee free public transit."

Affirmative value: justice, as giving each person fair access to public life. Criterion: removing barriers that fall hardest on people with the least money.

One argument: a fare is the same price for everyone but a much larger share of a small income, so it keeps the poorest riders off the bus first. Free transit removes that barrier for exactly the people the criterion names.

In a Crossfire drill with the AI asking, expect the question that matters: "If the city pays for free transit by cutting routes, has your criterion been met?" If you have no answer ready, the negative has its turn. Write the answer into your case before the round, not during the 1AR.

Lincoln-Douglas tool questions

Does DebateOwl run the LD cross-examinations?

Not inside a Sparring round. Sparring times the five speeches and skips both 3-minute cross-examinations, so practise those in Crossfire, which has a 3-minute setting and can question you or answer your questions.

Can AI write a value and criterion?

It can propose them. In DebateOwl, put the value and criterion you want in the debate's Instructions, since the generator reads them with the motion, then check that every argument comes back to the criterion. A criterion no argument uses is a label, not a test.
