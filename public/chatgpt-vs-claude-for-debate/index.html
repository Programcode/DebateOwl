What general chatbots are good at in debate

Debate Ladder's guide to ChatGPT lists jobs a general chatbot does well: brainstorming both sides, steel-manning the other side, rebuttal drills, and questions you might be asked in cross-examination. Those are real uses. Treat the output as a draft, and verify every citation before it goes into a speech. S2

Where Debate Ladder's guide says they struggle

The same guide says that in long timed rounds a general chatbot drifts or softens, the difficulty is not calibrated, scoring is inconsistent, and there is no progress record. That is Debate Ladder's description of ChatGPT, not a claim we measured ourselves. A long round is easier in a tool that already knows the speech times. S2

Prompt pack: make any chatbot argue back

These five prompts are plain-language instructions you can paste into ChatGPT or Claude. They do not replace a saved case.

1. Hold the other side

Take the other side of this motion and do not concede unless my answer beats your point. Stay on that side for the whole exchange.

2. Five things they will say

List the 5 things the other side will say about this motion. Give each one a one-line answer I could say aloud.

3. One question at a time

Cross-examine me with one question at a time. Wait for my answer before you ask the next question.

4. The weakest reason

Read this argument and find the weakest reason in it. Say what a judge would still be unsure about.

5. The second speech

Write a second speech: no new arguments, about 2 minutes backing up our arguments, then "If they say X, we answer Y" for each of their points.

DebateOwl's case generator writes this shape for you, including prompt 5's second speech, and you can regenerate one weak section without rewriting the rest.

A week that uses all three

Monday is for the chatbot. Paste the motion into ChatGPT and ask for the five arguments each side might run, then ask Claude to steel-man the side you understand less. Keep that list. Do not paste it into a speech yet. The job of Monday is to see the field, not to memorise a paragraph.

Tuesday is DebateOwl. Start a Current Debate with the motion and your side. Generate the case and read it aloud once, slowly, before you edit anything. Mark the argument you would be unhappy to say in front of a judge. Regenerate that section only. The replies at the bottom are the start of Tuesday night: pick the two you think the other side will actually run, and say the answer out loud in one breath.

Wednesday is evidence. Take one factual sentence from the case, ask Factoids for a lead, and open the source. If the source does not say what the case said, cut the sentence. A chatbot can suggest search terms for the same claim. It should not be the thing you quote.

Thursday is Crossfire. Ten minutes of one question at a time is enough. Write down the question that made you pause. That pause is the line to fix in the case, not a reason to add a sixth argument.

Friday is the timed round. Sparring in the format you actually debate, typed or spoken. Read the verdict for the reason it names, then change one thing. The free timer is there if you are drilling a format Sparring does not simulate, such as British Parliamentary or World Schools. Saturday is a human, if you have one. Sunday is off. The $5 month is for the weeks that look like this, not for a subscription you forget about in June.

Questions about chatbots and debate

Is ChatGPT or Claude better for debate?

Neither is clearly better for everyone. ChatGPT is a fast way to brainstorm, and Claude is a strong place to paste a long case and ask for a critique. DebateOwl is the pick when you also want the case shape, the replies and a timed round without rebuilding that setup.

Can ChatGPT run a debate round with me?

It can role-play an opponent if you set it up. It has no built-in speech timer or verdict for debate formats, so long rounds work better in a tool built for them.

Is Claude free?

Yes. Claude has a free plan. Pro is $20/month billed monthly, or $17/month billed annually.

Will AI make up sources?

It can, so open every source and confirm the exact claim before it goes on a card.
