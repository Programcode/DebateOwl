Is Prilo or DebateOwl better value for debate practice?

Both cover Lincoln-Douglas and Public Forum. Prilo NSDA adds Policy, a 3-minute cross-examination simulator, speech analytics and an evidence library, for $15 a month or $60 a year. DebateOwl costs $5 a month after a 3-day free trial, works in English, Spanish and Hungarian, writes a whole case with "If they say… we answer…" replies, and runs full timed rounds in School 4-6-6-3, Lincoln-Douglas and Public Forum that end in a judge's verdict. Pick Prilo if…

…you compete on the American circuit in Lincoln-Douglas, Public Forum or Policy and want a drill for each skill a tournament tests: a 3-minute cross-examination against the clock, flowing at speeds that build up to nationals pace, feedback on your pace and filler words, and a library for cutting evidence cards. Policy debaters especially: DebateOwl has no Policy practice round. If you will train all season, its $60 yearly plan costs a third of twelve monthly payments.

Pick DebateOwl if…

…you want the whole case written and then a whole round to say it in, from the first speech to the last, with a verdict that tells you what to fix. Pick it too if you debate in Spanish or Hungarian, debate the School 4-6-6-3 format, or want to try before you pay: the trial asks for no card, and the plan is monthly, with no year to commit to.

Reading the ledger fairly

Prilo's tools are drills: each takes one part of a round and repeats it. DebateOwl's centre is the round: the case comes first, the questions next, the full timed round last. A debater with both would use Prilo for flowing and pace, and DebateOwl for the case and the full run.

Prilo price questions

Does Prilo have a free trial?

Its NSDA page and sign-up page, as of November 2026, name none: you create an account, pick monthly or yearly, and go to checkout. Prilo's free NSDA resources, such as event guides and topic analysis, need no plan.

Which is cheaper over a year?

They come out level. Prilo's yearly plan is $60, paid once. DebateOwl has only a monthly plan, and twelve months at $5 is also $60, plus tax, after the 3-day trial.

Is Prilo part of the NSDA?

No. Prilo's own page says it is built by students and not affiliated with the National Speech & Debate Association.
