Should I use DebateOwl or ArguFight?

Pick DebateOwl if a set motion and a date are coming: it writes a case you can read out, "If they say… we answer…" replies, and runs timed practice rounds against an AI at real speech times. Pick ArguFight if you want to argue in writing against real people, have a panel of AI judges score each debate, and climb a ranking, or if you debate in a language DebateOwl does not offer. ArguFight is free to start. Plenty of debaters could use both: prep in one, test the arguments on strangers in the other.

What ArguFight is, in its own words

As of October 2026, ArguFight calls itself an AI-judged debate platform. You create a challenge, choose FOR or AGAINST, and pick standard mode (5 rounds over 24 hours) or speed mode (3 rounds in 1 hour). Someone accepts from the open challenges, you trade written arguments round by round, and spectators can watch. S1

Up to three AI judges score each debate from 0 to 100 and explain their reasoning, and the majority decides. Its pages count the roster differently: the judges page shows ten named personas with published paradigms, the homepage says both "3 of 7" and "up to 3" from six, and the About page says six. Every verdict also comes with coaching notes on what worked and what to fix. S1 S2 S3

Around that sit ELO rankings, championship belts in categories such as Science and Politics, bracket tournaments, community judging, a fact-check that the judges never see, speech-to-text input, and an AI opponent you can warm up against. The site lists 26 languages, including Spanish and Hungarian. It says it is free to start and free forever for base debating. S1

What DebateOwl is for

DebateOwl starts from a motion you have been given. You open a Current Debate for it, and the case, sources, drills and practice rounds stay filed under that motion. The case generator writes the speech in the order you would give it. Attack looks for the weak link in the other side, Factoids suggests evidence with a source you still have to open, Crossfire drills questions, and Sparring runs a timed round against an AI in School 4-6-6-3, Lincoln-Douglas or Public Forum, typed or spoken, ending with a verdict. World Schools and British Parliamentary get the prep tools, not a simulated round.

One motion, prepared here and tested there

Motion: "Social media apps should be banned for under-16s." Side: for. This is the shape DebateOwl gives you, with the evidence left as what to look for, because that part you check yourself.

  1. Opening. We do not let fourteen-year-olds sign phone contracts alone. We should not let them sign up to apps built to keep them scrolling.
  2. Argument 1: the design works against young users. Explanation: endless feeds and streaks reward time spent, not time well spent. Evidence: find a study on teenage sleep and late-night app use; note the author and year. Why it matters: lost sleep hits schoolwork and mood every day, not only on bad days.
  3. Argument 2: parents cannot police it alone. Explanation: one family's rules collapse when the whole class is on the app. Evidence: find a survey of parents on setting screen-time rules. Why it matters: a legal age gives every family the same line to point to.
  4. If they say teenagers will lie about their age, we answer: some will, as with any age limit, and the rule still moves the default for everyone else.
  5. If they say social media keeps isolated teenagers connected, we answer: messaging and video calls stay open; the ban targets feeds built to hold attention.
  6. Closing. A good age limit does not stop teenagers growing up online. It stops the apps deciding how fast.

To test it on ArguFight, post the motion as a speed challenge. Your first round can carry the opening and argument 1, and the "If they say…" lines are ready for whatever the other person writes back. ArguFight's judges then tell you which point held. That split of rounds is our suggestion, not an ArguFight rule.

  • Real human opponents. DebateOwl has none.
  • A free start, with no trial clock.

ArguFight questions

Is ArguFight free?

As of October 2026 its homepage says it is free to start and "free forever for base debating", and its schools page says classrooms are free. Its /pricing address returned 404, so any paid tiers are not stated.

Does ArguFight write a case for me?

Not that its pages say. You write each round's argument yourself; the AI judges score it and explain the verdict. DebateOwl is the one that drafts a case with replies.

Can I debate real people on DebateOwl?

No. DebateOwl's practice rounds are against an AI opponent. ArguFight is built around human opponents, with AI opponents for warming up.

Which is better for a teacher?

For a class tournament that an AI judges, ArguFight for Schools is free and lets you make it private. For a live debate in the classroom, DebateOwl's free timer, motion generator and flow sheet need no student accounts.

Which languages do they support?

ArguFight's site lists 26 languages. The DebateOwl app is in English, Spanish and Hungarian.
