Should I prepare my debate with Claude or with DebateOwl?

Use DebateOwl when the motion and the date are fixed: it keeps the motion, your side and the speech times in one place, writes the case in the order you will say it with "If they say… we answer…" replies, and times a practice round that ends in a judge's verdict. Claude is a strong general assistant with a free plan, Projects that remember instructions, cited web search and voice mode. It is enough when you want ideas, a critique of a case you already wrote, or a spoken chat without a clock.

Tonight's motion

"Schools should replace graded homework with supervised study time."

You are Pro, speaking first, in a Public Forum round on Friday. Below, the same evening runs twice, step by step: once in a Claude chat, once in DebateOwl. Neither column is a test result. Both describe what each product's own pages and screens offer as of October 2026.

One rule for both columns: open every source and find the sentence before it goes in your speech. A citation proves a page exists, not that it says what your case needs.

The evening, six steps, two tools

  1. 1Set up the motion

    In a Claude chat

    Make a Project for the motion and put the motion, your side and the format in its instructions. Claude follows those instructions in every chat inside the Project, and the free plan allows five Projects. C2

    In DebateOwl

    Open a Current Debate with the motion, your side and your speaker role. Every case, source, drill and round you make tonight is filed under it, and none of it leaks into your other motions.

  2. 2Write the case

    In a Claude chat

    Ask for a Pro case. Claude will write one, but its shape is whatever you ask for, so spell it out: an opening, the arguments, the evidence, answers to the other side, a closing.

    In DebateOwl

    The case generator writes it in speaking order: an opening line, two to four arguments that each carry an explanation, evidence naming its source and year, and why it matters, then replies and a closing. A weak argument can be rewritten on its own.

  3. 3Back it with evidence

    In a Claude chat

    Turn on web search and ask for studies on homework and grades. Each answer that uses search comes with citations. C3

    In DebateOwl

    Factoids looks up a claim and suggests evidence with a named source.

  4. 4Get ready for their replies

    In a Claude chat

    Ask what the Con side will say, or tell Claude to argue Con for the whole chat and not give in until your answer beats its point.

    In DebateOwl

    The case already ends with "If they say… we answer…" lines. Attack reads the Con case for its weakest link, and Crossfire either questions you one question at a time or lets you question it.

  5. 5Say it against the clock

    In a Claude chat

    Voice mode gives you a full spoken conversation on every plan. Its pages say nothing about a speech timer, so put your phone timer next to it and keep the Public Forum times yourself. C4

    In DebateOwl

    Sparring runs a Public Forum round at its real times: four-minute constructives and rebuttals, three-minute summaries, a two-minute final focus. Speak or type; in a voice round the opponent answers aloud. It ends with a judge's verdict, a score per category and what to work on next. Delivery is judged from your words, not your voice.

  6. 6The next morning

    In a Claude chat

    The chats sit inside the Project. To take the case into the round, copy it out into a document.

    In DebateOwl

    The case and the round transcript are under the motion. Export the case to Google Docs or PDF and print it.

When plain Claude is enough

  • You are exploring a motion you have never argued. A free chat that lists both sides is all you need on day one.
  • You already wrote the case. Paste it in or upload the file and ask Claude where a judge would stop believing you. That critique is one of its strengths.
  • You want to talk an argument through out loud with no format and no clock. Voice mode does that on the free plan.
  • Your round is a class discussion, not a timed debate with set speeches.

Claude questions, answered

Is Claude free?

Yes. As of October 2026 Claude has a $0 plan with usage limits that reset every five hours and every week. Pro is $20 a month, or $17 a month billed annually.

Can Claude time my speeches?

The Claude pages we read describe no speech timer. Voice mode holds a spoken conversation; keeping each speech to its minutes is up to you.

Does Claude work in Hungarian?

Claude's interface comes in eleven languages, Spanish among them and Hungarian not, and its help page says you can still chat in any language. The DebateOwl app is in English, Spanish and Hungarian.
