Speech lengths, cross-examination and prep time for the main formats, and how to practise against the clock.

How long are debate speeches in each format?

It depends on the format. Public Forum has 4-minute constructives and rebuttals, 3-minute summaries and 2-minute final focus speeches. Lincoln-Douglas runs 6, 7, 4, 6 and 3 minutes. Policy uses 8-minute constructives and 5-minute rebuttals. British Parliamentary speeches are 7 minutes, and World Schools speeches are 8, followed by 4-minute replies. Cross-examination usually takes 3 minutes, and prep time varies by league, so check your tournament's rules. The free DebateOwl debate timer loads each of these speech orders for you.

Timing changes strategy

A strong argument still loses if it arrives too late or runs too long. Speaking against the clock forces the choices a round will force anyway: which argument goes first, which one gets cut, and how much time the weighing needs.

Most debaters overestimate how much they can say in a minute. A timed run of a speech you thought was four minutes long usually shows where the time actually goes, and that is the first thing to fix.

Speech times by format

These are the speech lengths under each format's most common rules. Leagues and tournaments change them, so check the invitation before you practise.

  • Public Forum (NSDA): two 4-minute constructives, two 4-minute rebuttals, two 3-minute summaries and two 2-minute final focus speeches, with three 3-minute crossfires and 3 minutes of prep per team.
  • Lincoln-Douglas (NSDA): 6-minute affirmative constructive, 7-minute negative constructive, 4-minute first affirmative rebuttal, 6-minute negative rebuttal and 3-minute second affirmative rebuttal, with two 3-minute cross-examinations and 4 minutes of prep per debater.
  • Policy (NSDA): four 8-minute constructives, each followed by 3 minutes of cross-examination, then four 5-minute rebuttals, with 8 minutes of prep per team.
  • British Parliamentary (WUDC): eight 7-minute speeches. Points of information are allowed except in the first and last minute, and teams prepare for 15 minutes before the round.
  • World Schools (WSDC): six 8-minute substantive speeches with the first and last minute protected, then two 4-minute reply speeches, Opposition first.

Drills to run against the clock

  1. Thirty-second blocks. Deliver one prepared rebuttal block in thirty seconds. If it runs over, cut the setup, not the response.
  2. Full speech. Give a whole constructive at its real length. Note where the one-minute beep lands; if you are still on your first argument, the structure needs work.
  3. Short-clock rebuttal. Give a rebuttal with a minute less than the format allows. The real speech will then feel like it has room for weighing.
  4. Cross-ex timing. Run a three-minute question period against the clock and count how many questions actually led somewhere.
  5. Full round order. Step through the whole format with a partner, using the arrow keys to move between speakers, so you practise the gaps as well as the speeches.

Drill one skill per session. A timer shows how long a speech took, not why, so the next step matters as much as the timing.

Pair timing with feedback

After each timed speech, write down three things: where the one-minute signal caught you, what you cut, and what you would cut next time. Then fix one of them and run the speech again.

For feedback on the content, run the same speech in a Sparring round in DebateOwl. The verdict says which arguments carried the round and which ones the judge did not weigh, which is usually where the time went.

Frequently asked questions

How long is a debate speech?

Between two and eight minutes, depending on the format and the speech. Public Forum final focus speeches are the shortest at two minutes; Policy constructives and World Schools substantive speeches run eight.

Which formats does the free debate timer support?

Public Forum, Lincoln-Douglas, Policy, British Parliamentary, World Schools, Asian Parliamentary and the school 4-6-6-3 format, and you can build your own. The timer inside the DebateOwl app has presets for Standard 4-6-6-3, Lincoln-Douglas, Public Forum and Policy.

Does the timer include prep time or cross-examination?

The free debate timer does: it times cross-examination and crossfire as part of the round and keeps a prep bank for each side in Public Forum, Lincoln-Douglas and Policy. The timer inside the DebateOwl app times speeches only.

Can I use the timer without looking at the screen?

Yes. It beeps once at one minute remaining and twice at time up, and you can turn the sound on or off.

What keyboard shortcuts does the timer have?

Space starts and stops the clock, R resets it, and the arrow keys change speaker.

How often should I practise against a timer?

Every time you practise a speech. Short timed drills several times a week build pacing faster than one long session.
