Practice With an Online Debate Timer

Train pacing, prep discipline, and speech structure before the round starts.

How should I use a timer to practise debate speeches?

Time every practice speech at its real length, because most debaters overestimate how much they can say in a minute. Note where the one-minute signal catches you, what you cut and what you would cut next time, then fix one thing and run the speech again. Short drills such as thirty-second blocks and short-clock rebuttals build pacing quickly. DebateLab's timer has speech presets for Standard 4-6-6-3, Lincoln-Douglas, Public Forum and Policy. It does not time cross-examination, prep time, World Schools or British Parliamentary, so a phone timer covers those.

Format presets

Speech orders and times for Standard 4-6-6-3, Lincoln-Douglas, Public Forum and Policy, so each speech starts with the right clock.

Signals you can hear

One beep at one minute remaining and two beeps at time up, so you can practise without watching the screen.

Keyboard control

Space starts and stops the clock, R resets it, and the arrow keys move to the next or previous speaker.

Timing changes strategy

A strong argument still loses if it arrives too late or runs too long. Speaking against the clock forces the choices a round will force anyway: which argument goes first, which one gets cut, and how much time the weighing needs.

Most debaters overestimate how much they can say in a minute. A timed run of a speech you thought was four minutes long usually shows where the time actually goes, and that is the first thing to fix.

What the DebateLab timer covers

Choose a format and the timer loads its speech order. Each speech has its own length, and moving to the next speaker resets the clock for that speech.

  • Standard (4-6-6-3): introductor, two refutation speeches and a conclusor.
  • Lincoln-Douglas: 6-minute affirmative constructive, 7-minute negative constructive, 4-minute first affirmative rebuttal, 6-minute negative rebuttal and 3-minute second affirmative rebuttal.
  • Public Forum: four 4-minute constructive and rebuttal speeches, two 3-minute summaries and two 2-minute final focus speeches.
  • Policy: four 8-minute constructives and four 5-minute rebuttals.

The presets cover speeches only. Cross-examination periods, crossfire and prep time are not on the clock, so time those separately. World Schools and British Parliamentary have no preset; their speeches run 8 and 7 minutes, and a phone timer covers them.

Drills to run against the clock

  1. Thirty-second blocks. Deliver one prepared rebuttal block in thirty seconds. If it runs over, cut the setup, not the response.
  2. Full speech. Give a whole constructive at its real length. Note where the one-minute beep lands; if you are still on your first argument, the structure needs work.
  3. Short-clock rebuttal. Give a rebuttal with a minute less than the format allows. The real speech will then feel like it has room for weighing.
  4. Cross-ex timing. Run a three-minute question period on a separate timer and count how many questions actually led somewhere.
  5. Full round order. Step through the whole format with a partner, using the arrow keys to move between speakers, so you practise the gaps as well as the speeches.

Drill one skill per session. A timer shows how long a speech took, not why, so the next step matters as much as the timing.

Pair timing with feedback

After each timed speech, write down three things: where the one-minute signal caught you, what you cut, and what you would cut next time. Then fix one of them and run the speech again.

For feedback on the content, run the same speech in a Sparring round in DebateLab. The verdict says which arguments carried the round and which ones the judge did not weigh, which is usually where the time went.

Frequently asked questions

Which debate formats does the timer support?

Standard 4-6-6-3, Lincoln-Douglas, Public Forum and Policy. World Schools and British Parliamentary have no preset yet, so time those speeches separately.

Does the timer include prep time or cross-examination?

No. The presets time speeches only. Use a second timer for cross-examination, crossfire and prep time.

Can I use the timer without looking at the screen?

Yes. It beeps once at one minute remaining and twice at time up, and you can turn the sound on or off.

What keyboard shortcuts does the timer have?

Space starts and stops the clock, R resets it, and the arrow keys change speaker.

How often should I practise against a timer?

Every time you practise a speech. Short timed drills several times a week build pacing faster than one long session.
