What makes a rebuttal effective

A good rebuttal does not answer every sentence. It attacks the part of the argument that matters: the warrant, evidence quality, internal link, impact, or weighing comparison.

DebateLab helps identify those pressure points so your prep produces sharper responses.

Train with your own cases

Paste a case, speech, or contention and ask what a strong opponent would attack. Then build blocks that answer the most likely objections.

This is especially useful when preparing alone and you do not have a teammate available to pressure-test your arguments.

Use cross-examination as a weapon

Weak warrants often become strong cross-examination questions. DebateLab can help turn hidden assumptions into questions that expose the other side's burden.

Frequently asked questions

Can DebateLab analyze my argument?

Yes. You can use it to find logical gaps, missing weighing, and likely attacks.

Does rebuttal practice work for any format?

The principles apply broadly, though you should adapt timing and style to your event.

Can it suggest cross-examination questions?

Yes. Weak assumptions can be turned into pressure questions.
